The problem

The phone rings. Again.

A busy neighborhood restaurant takes most of its takeout orders the old way: a host with a phone pinned between shoulder and ear, scribbling on a pad while the dining room waits. Every Friday rush, the same story — missed orders, misheard names, and a line forming at the host stand.

The "easy" fix is a delivery marketplace. The catch: 15–30% of every sale walks out the door in commissions, and the customer relationship — the emails, the repeat visits — belongs to the app, not the restaurant.

The brief: give the restaurant its own ordering engine — something a website or mobile app can talk to, that the owner fully controls, cheap to run and easy to extend. No per-order tax.

  • 30% Commission ceiling on marketplace orders — per sale, forever
  • 2× Staff time lost: taking the call and re-keying it for the kitchen
  • 0 Customer emails the restaurant owns when ordering through an app

The approach

Nine endpoints, one job each

A small Flask service split into two clean domains — the menu and the orders — plus a self-documenting /api/docs route, so any front-end or kitchen display can integrate without a manual.

menuMenu management — full CRUD

  • GET/api/menuList dishes · ?category=main filters
  • POST/api/menuCreate a dish — name, price, category validated
  • GET/api/menu/<id>Fetch one dish
  • PUT/api/menu/<id>Replace a dish (full payload)
  • DELETE/api/menu/<id>Remove a dish — 204 on success

ordersOrder intake & kitchen status

  • GET/api/ordersList orders · ?status=preparing filters
  • POST/api/ordersCreate an order — prices resolved server-side, totals computed
  • GET/api/orders/<id>Fetch one order with line items
  • PATCH/api/orders/<id>Advance status: received → preparing → ready → completed

Prices never trust the client

Orders reference menu ids only. The server resolves each dish, checks availability, and computes line totals — a tampered request can't invent a discount.

Errors are always JSON

Every failure — validation, missing record, wrong method — returns {"error": "…"} with the right status. Front-ends can show them to users directly; nothing ever comes back as an HTML error page.

Demo in minutes, grow later

Ships with 12 realistic seeded dishes and a single dependency (Flask). Storage is in-memory for the demo and swaps for SQLite or Postgres without changing the API contract.
